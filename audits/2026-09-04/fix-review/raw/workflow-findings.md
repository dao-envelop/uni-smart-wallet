### Shipped oracleMaxSpotDeviationBps = 50 is below the basis this repo itself measured on Unichain; the mandatory pre-deploy recalibration is absent from the runbook and the justifying comment cites only the quiet snapshot

**Location:** script/chain_params.json:18,44,101,117,154; script/DeployStableLP.s.sol:57-60; script/RUNBOOK-2026-09-05.md (Предпосылки / per-chain checklist, :161-162); tasks/oracle_maxdeviation_analysis.md snapshot 2

The branch ships `"oracleMaxSpotDeviationBps": 50` explicitly on chains 1, 130, 8453, 42161 and 1301, and every other chain entry in `chain_params.json` carries `"oracle": true` and inherits the same 50 from `DEFAULT_ORACLE_MAX_SPOT_DEVIATION_BPS`. `tasks/oracle_maxdeviation_analysis.md` snapshot 2 (2026-07-21) records **Unichain ETH/UNI basis 72 bps and ETH/WBTC 39 bps** — 72 is above the shipped tolerance on a calm day, and 39 leaves 11 bps. This is a liveness failure only — `_guardSwap` returns immediately for `byOwner` (src/BaseLPManager.sol:355), so the NFT owner can allocate, recenter, move and withdraw throughout, and `claimFees` is not gated on either product. A future maintainer sizing this parameter is pointed at exactly the subset of the data that fits.

## F-3 [MEDIUM] (confidence high)
### The documented residual omits the pool-vs-feed basis, and the README's sizing rule is unsatisfiable and wrong in the loosening direction

**Location:** script/README.md:193-196; script/DeployStableLP.s.sol:57-60; tasks/task_053_operator_add_spot_guard.md:230-232; src/oracle/ChainlinkPriceOracle.sol:289-297

Three documents state the post-fix residual as extraction within `maxSpotDeviationBps − poolFee` per operation. The guard, however, bounds the pool against the **Chainlink reference**, not against the price the pool reverts to. Writing the reference as `market·(1+b)`, an attacker choosing the sign that helps reaches `|b| + D` of mispricing, so the correct expression is `basis + maxSpotDeviationBps − poolFee`. The repo measured `b` at 16-33 bps on the mainnet pools and 26-72 bps on Arbitrum/Unichain, so the documented figure understates the accepted residual by roughly 1.7x on mainnet ETH/WBTC 0.05% (78 vs 45 bps) and up to ~4.6x on Unichain ETH/UNI 0.30% (92 vs 20 bps). This is a magnitude correction to a residual the task already accepts as deliberate and repeatable — it does not open a new path, and it leaves per-operation exposure well under 1% of redeployed principal on the configured mainnet set, versus the PoC's −22.4%/−44.5%.

The part that will cause a future mistake is `script/README.md:196`: "a spot tolerance at or above the fee tier of the thinnest configured pool gives the guard nothing to do". With the basis term restored, zero residual requires `D < poolFee − basis`, which is negative on every configured pool whose basis exceeds its fee tier — mainnet ETH/WBTC (33 vs 5), ETH/USDT (24 vs 5), WBTC/USDC (17 vs 5), Unichain ETH/UNI (72 vs 30). No positive tolerance eliminates the residual on those pools; the guard bounds it, it cannot remove it. The rule as written tells a deployer that a sub-fee-tier tolerance would make the guard airtight, and simultaneously implies that anything above the fee tier is pointless — a reading that would encourage either an unsatisfiably tight setting (which fail-closes all operator activity) or dismissing the guard entirely. Both are wrong: at the shipped 50 bps on a 30 bps pool the guard still cuts the PoC's drain from ~4450 bps to ~20-90 bps per operation. The same paragraph is the sizing guidance the deployer will read alongside F-1's number.

Relatedly, `tasks/task_053_operator_add_spot_guard.md:77-79` argues "at 50 bps on a 0.30% pool ~0.2% remains, on a 1% pool the attack is unprofitable". With the basis folded in, the unprofitability conclusion flips wherever `b > 50` — a value the repo measured on Unichain. That is a qualitative error in the fix's own profitability argument, not just a magnitude one.

**Recommendation:** Doc-and-parameter work only; zero manager bytes. (1) State the residual as `basis + maxSpotDeviationBps − poolFee` in script/README.md:193-195, task_053 and the CHANGELOG. (2) Replace the fee-tier rule at README:196 with the sizing rule that actually applies: the tolerance must exceed a high percentile of the measured basis of the widest configured pool (or operator ops fail closed), the residual it leaves cannot be driven to zero on any pool whose basis exceeds its fee tier, and the guard's job is to kill the 20-80% skews, not to police the last 50 bps. (3) Correct the task's "unprofitable on a 1% pool" claim. (4) Optionally add a per-pool tolerance override on the oracle (`mapping(PoolId => uint16)` + owner setter, ~150-300 B on a contract with 18,675 B of margin, 0 B in any manager) so stable pairs can run at 10-20 bps while a lagged pair runs wider. Does not block, but ship the doc corrections in the same commit as the parameter decision from F-1.

## F-5 [LOW] (confidence high)
### The fail-closed surface widened past what the docs disclose: no-feed pools, a cheap third-party skew, and the L2 sequencer grace period now stop operator liquidity ops, not just swaps

**Location:** src/oracle/ChainlinkPriceOracle.sol:227,296-297,326-334; src/BaseLPManager.sol:351-359; script/README.md:180-192

`script/README.md:180-190` documents two consequences of the widening — a pool with no feed becomes entirely operator-inert, and a market move outrunning the heartbeat blocks operator ops until the feed catches up — and calls the second the intended trade. Two further consequences are not disclosed.

**The sequencer gate now governs liquidity.** `check` opens with `if (!_sequencerUp()) return false` (:227), and `_sequencerUp` (:326-334) requires the uptime feed to read "up" **and** `block.timestamp - startedAt > sequencerGracePeriod`, defaulted to 3600 s. Before this fix that gate only stopped operator swaps; now, on Base and Arbitrum, for a full hour after any sequencer restart every operator `allocate`, `reinvest`, `recenter` and `moveLiquidity` reverts `OperatorSwapUnverified` — which is exactly the window after a restart in which positions are most likely to have drifted out of range. Fail-closed is the right default here, but it is a new operational property on the two chains that have an operator running, and it appears in no runbook or README line.

**Third-party griefing is cheaper than before.** Any account that pushes a configured pool more than `maxSpotDeviationBps` off the reference denies every operator liquidity op in that pool until ordinary flow or a feed update closes the gap. This existed for swap-bearing operator legs since task_031; it now covers swapless `recenter`, `moveLiquidity`, `allocate` and `reinvest`, which previously had no price precondition at all (the branch's own inverted tests pin the change: `test_operatorSwaplessRecenter_noOracle_succeeds` → `_reverts`). Persistence is bounded, contrary to how this is sometimes stated: arbitrage is priced against the market and pays the fee once, so only a nudge no larger than the pool's own fee tier is free to hold — which requires the standing basis to have already consumed most of the tolerance, i.e. the F-1 condition. The attacker profits nothing (a reverted operator tx yields nothing extractable) and must keep paying to hold it. The operator also has an in-transaction remedy on the swap-bearing paths: a corrective pre-swap in the same pool executes better than the reference-implied price and clears the 100 bps swap gate by construction, unblocking its own add.

Bound on all of it: the NFT owner bypasses the guard entirely (BaseLPManager.sol:355) and can allocate, recenter, move and `withdrawTo` at any time; `claimFees` is ungated on both products; nothing is stuck or seizable. The loss is operator automation and the yield foregone while a drifted position goes unrecentered.

**Recommendation:** Add both to `script/README.md`'s consequence list (:183-190) and to the runbook: an L2 sequencer restart suspends all operator liquidity operations for `sequencerGracePeriod`, and a pool can be pushed into an operator-inert state by anyone at roughly the round-trip fee when the standing basis is close to the tolerance — the remedy being an owner transaction or waiting out the gap. Sizing the tolerance off a percentile (F-1) rather than a quiet snapshot is what makes the griefing push cost real money instead of a few bps. Zero bytes anywhere.

## F-7 [LOW] (confidence high)
### The documented remediation for a stale circuit-breaker cache is a no-op: SetOracleFeeds skips exactly the case a Chainlink phase change produces, and a test pins the skip as correct

**Location:** src/oracle/ChainlinkPriceOracle.sol:155-170; script/SetOracleFeeds.s.sol:82-93; test/SetOracleFeeds.t.sol (idempotence case)

`setFeed` is the only writer of `feedBounds`, and its natspec says: "Chainlink can swap the aggregator behind a proxy (a phase change), which leaves the cache stale — re-run `SetOracleFeeds` after one." But `applyFeeds` skips a currency whose stored `(aggregator, heartbeat, tokenDecimals)` already matches (script/SetOracleFeeds.s.sol:85-89), and a phase change swaps the underlying aggregator *behind* the registered proxy, leaving all three identical. The prescribed refresh prints "skip (current)" and writes nothing. `test/SetOracleFeeds.t.sol` asserts the skip as desired behaviour, so the gap is actively pinned.

The skip tuple is wrong on two cached values, not one: `setFeed` also caches `feedDecimals` from `IAggregatorV3(aggregator).decimals()` (:162), which is a *pricing* input to both the swap branch (:262-263) and `_checkSpot` (:293). A phase change that moved decimals would silently mis-scale every comparison with the same no-op remediation. Chainlink keeps decimals stable across phases, so that half is theoretical; the bounds half is not.

Direction of harm, corrected from how this was first framed: Chainlink has deprecated min/maxAnswer on most feeds, and mainnet USDC currently reports the `2^176 − 1` sentinel (which `_readBounds` correctly drops as "no bound" because it exceeds `int128.max`), while Base/Arbitrum USDC still carry a legacy 1.05e8 ceiling. The plausible pending migration is therefore bounded → unbounded, which leaves the cache *tighter* than reality and fails **closed** — an operator add reverting during a >5% depeg. The fail-open direction (a stale zero bound while a new breaker is added) requires Chainlink to reverse its own deprecation, plus a depeg that pins the feed, plus a compromised operator, and would only return the oracle to its pre-fix state (audit L-3, rated LOW). Either way this is a hole in a defence-in-depth mitigation, not a live exploit.

The contract is not stuck: the oracle owner can refresh at any time with a direct `setFeed(currency, sameAggregator, sameHeartbeat, sameDecimals)`, which recomputes bounds unconditionally. Only the *documented* path cannot do it, and neither `script/README.md` nor the runbook mentions bounds at all.

**Recommendation:** Pick one, both cheap and both outside the EIP-170 budget. Either make `applyFeeds` compare `oracle.feedBounds(currency)` (and `feedDecimals`) against a fresh read of the resolved aggregator, or honour a `FORCE=1` env that disables the skip — and update test/SetOracleFeeds.t.sol accordingly. Alternatively add an owner-only `refreshBounds(Currency[])` to the oracle (~150-250 B against 18,675 B of headroom) and point the natspec at it. In every case correct the comment at ChainlinkPriceOracle.sol:156-157: as written it sends an operator down a path that provably does nothing. Does not block.

## F-8 [LOW] (confidence high)
### The oracle's sentinel-safety comment asserts an invariant StableLPManager._handleReinvest does not hold

**Location:** src/oracle/ChainlinkPriceOracle.sol:242-246; src/StableLPManager.sol:293-299 (vs :185-192 and src/VolatileLPManager.sol:222-228)

The comment that justifies the `amountIn == 0` sentinel — added by this branch — reads: "What makes the sentinel safe is that no real swap can wear it, guarded twice over: the swap paths reach `_guardSwap` only inside `if (swapAmountIn > 0)`, and what they pass is the *realized* input, which their full-fill check has already required to be no smaller than the requested one."

Of the three swap-path `_guardSwap` invocations, two carry the full-fill check (`StableLPManager:189`, `VolatileLPManager:224`, the latter shared by allocate, recenter and move) and one does not: `_handleReinvest` swaps at :294 and guards at :298 with no `SwapSlippage` revert between them. A zero realized input is reachable there without a revert — a hookless Stable pool whose price has drifted outside the manager's fixed range, with no other liquidity between spot and the operator-chosen `swapPriceLimit`, lets `SwapMath.computeSwapStep` walk the price to the limit consuming nothing — so half of the stated double guard is absent at that site.

The consequence today is benign: `inDelta == 0` implies `outDelta == 0` (nothing moved), the swap branch would be vacuous at zero magnitude anyway (`expectedOut = 0`, `minOut = 0`), and the check the call receives instead — `_checkSpot` — is symmetric and, at the shipped 100/50 configuration, tighter than the one it displaces. Note that last point is a deployment convention rather than a code invariant: both tolerances are independently owner-settable against the same 1000 bps cap, so an inverted configuration would make the sentinel path the weaker of the two and remove the accident that makes this harmless.

The defect worth acting on is the comment. A safety argument that survives by accident while claiming to survive by construction is what the next edit breaks — specifically, an engineer who believes the invariant holds everywhere will feel free to make the sentinel branch permit something the swap branch does not.

**Recommendation:** Two options, both cheap. Preferred: add the one-line mirror of StableLPManager.sol:189 after the reinvest swap — `if (uint256(uint128(-inDelta)) < leg.swapAmountIn) revert SwapSlippage(leg.poolId);` — an estimated 40-70 B against StableLPManager's 459 B of headroom, 0 B in Volatile/OpenVolatile, and the comment then becomes true as written. Otherwise amend ChainlinkPriceOracle.sol:242-246 to say the full-fill floor is present on the allocate and Volatile paths but not on Stable reinvest, and why that is safe there. Do not leave both as they stand. Does not block.

## Verified correct
Guard placement and threading: every `POOL_MANAGER.modifyLiquidity` call with a positive `liquidityDelta` is preceded by the spot guard — StableLPManager.sol:215 before :221, VolatileLPManager.sol:288 before :293 — and both sit after the `getSlot0`/`PoolUninitialized` check and before `liquidityFromAmounts`, so no add can be sized at an unvouched price. `byOwner` originates at `_isOwnerCall()` in each external entry point, travels through the unlock payload, and reaches both add sites on all five paths; the owner bypass (`if (byOwner) return`, BaseLPManager.sol:355) is the only way past. Removal paths (`_pullLiquidity` in recenter/move, `claimFees` via a zero-delta poke) are correctly left ungated — removing at a skewed price is attacker-negative and gating it would add a denial surface for no benefit.

Oracle arithmetic: `_checkSpot`'s two-step `mulDiv(sqrtP,sqrtP,Q96)` → `mulDiv(priceX96, WAD*10**td0, Q96*10**td1)` pool price and `mulDiv(a0,(10**fd1)*WAD, a1*(10**fd0))` reference match `script/PriceDeviation.s.sol` expression for expression, so the repo's own "basis" measurements are directly comparable to the shipped tolerance. The split is necessary (a single-expression `sqrtP²/2^192` overflows). Zero-guards on `sqrtP`, `poolPrice` and `refPrice` all return "no opinion" rather than reverting, which the manager then fail-closes for operators only. `zeroForOne` is correctly ignored in the spot branch and the reason is documented.

M-1 and L-3: the oracle is `Ownable2Step` (transferOwnership nominates only), both tolerances are validated against `MAX_DEVIATION_CAP = 1000` in a shared setter path, and `_readBounds` resolves the underlying aggregator via `proxy.aggregator()` with every step wrapped in `try`, discarding anything outside `int128` — which correctly drops Chainlink's `2^176 − 1` "no breaker" sentinel to zero. `_read` treats an answer at or beyond a cached bound as no reference, alongside non-positive answers, zero/future timestamps and heartbeat staleness.

Sentinel safety on the swap side: no real swap can wear `amountIn == 0` on the allocate or Volatile paths — both floor the realized input at the requested amount before guarding (the one exception is F-8).

## Open questions
Whether Stable should carry the gate at all. StableLPManager's ranges are fixed at `initialize` with no setter (verified: rangeOf is written once at :78), and its operator surface has no principal-removal path — so an operator there cannot open a narrow band at a skewed price or recycle principal, and H-1's demonstrated mechanism does not reach it in the form the PoC showed. Stable's exposure is idle balance plus realized fees deployed at a bad price into a fixed range. Nobody has quantified that, and the Stable arm of the fix carries real availability and byte cost. A Stable arm of the PoC would settle it.

The basis distribution. Everything in F-1 and F-3 rests on two single-block snapshots taken 2026-07-20/21, which the analysis document itself calls too small a sample. Live re-reads attempted during this audit disagreed with each other (one lens reported Unichain ETH/UNI at 171 bps, another at 102, the repo's snapshot says 72), which is itself evidence that the quantity is volatile and that no single reading should set the parameter. Until `PriceDeviation.s.sol` is re-run over a volatile window on all four measurable chains, the correct value of `oracleMaxSpotDeviationBps` per chain is genuinely unknown — and that is exactly the step task_053 made a precondition and the runbook omits.

How often the fail-closed window actually bites. The duty cycle of a >50 bps basis on Unichain volatile pairs is unmeasured. With 24 h SVR heartbeats it could be a large fraction of the day on a trending asset, or rare. That distinction decides whether F-1 is "raise the number on one chain" or "do not run operators on those pools".

Whether a second `IPriceOracle` implementation is contemplated. F-4's fail-open branch is latent today because the only implementation is correct. It becomes live the moment anyone writes a Pyth/Redstone/second-source variant against the published interface. If the answer is "never", the natspec fix is hygiene; if "eventually", it is the difference between a documented contract and an undefined one.
